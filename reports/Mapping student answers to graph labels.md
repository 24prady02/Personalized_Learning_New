# Facet entailment, not bigger models, fits ES-Viterbi

No published system solves this exact problem: reading free-text explanations of Java code, matching each sentence to a typed knowledge-graph edge whose label is a sentence, deciding whether the student takes the correct side or the wrong side, and decoding concept levels with Viterbi. The researchers found **no paper that NLI-matches CS student explanations against a misconception catalogue**, and **none that runs Viterbi over a named misconception or learning-progression graph using text-matching emissions**. The closest prior art splits into three layers. **Nielsen's facet entailment** from SemEval-2013 is closest for edge reading. It judges each (term, relation, term) triple of a reference answer as Expressed, Contradicted or Unaddressed, and a non-LLM vote of exact match, WordNet and entailment reached about **0.78–0.82 weighted F1 per facet**. **AutoTutor's expectation–misconception matching** is the closest deterministic, label-free precedent for wrong-belief versus correct-rule pairs. **Andes-style Bayesian networks and BKT** are the precedent for inference over the graph with parameters set by hand. The literature points to one fix for the collapse from 16/19 to 5/19 levels when code execution was removed. The fix is not a stronger side-decider. It is to rebuild the level emissions from text-only evidence and calibrate them on the 365 overall-level answers, because levels no longer have the execution signal they were tuned on. The recommended method is facet-style three-way edge judgement with a margin against both twin texts, using an off-the-shelf NLI model with natural logic as a precision override. That judgement feeds a small number of emission parameters fitted on the 365 answers, with decoding constrained by the graph.

## Facet entailment is the published twin of "edge logic as label"

The SemEval-2013 Task 7 partial-entailment pilot is the nearest published version of reading a graph edge from student text. Reference answers were decomposed into **facets: two key terms plus a relation**, such as "contains – seeds" with relation Material. Each facet was judged Expressed or Unaddressed for each student answer, over a pilot test set of **16,263 facets** ([Dzikovska et al. 2013](https://aclanthology.org/S13-2045.pdf)). The earlier annotation scheme also had a Contradicted label, which maps onto the project's wrong-side decision, and humans agreed on facet labels **86.2%** of the time ([Nielsen et al. 2008](https://preview.aclanthology.org/author-url/L08-1166)). The only pilot participant, UKP-BIU, used no LLM. It voted over three methods: "Exact" (both facet words appear among the lemmatised student tokens), "WordNet" (the words or their WordNet relatives appear) and the BIUTEE entailment engine ([Zesch et al. 2013](https://aclanthology.org/S13-2048.pdf)). The majority vote reached **micro-F1 0.782 / 0.765 / 0.816** on unseen answers, unseen questions and unseen domains, against baselines of 0.670 / 0.688 / 0.731. A simple aggregation rule turned facet judgements into a whole-answer decision ([Levy et al. 2013](https://aclanthology.org/P13-2080.pdf)).

This matters for ES-Viterbi in three ways. First, a per-edge accuracy near 0.8 is the realistic ceiling for non-LLM methods on short science answers. TF-IDF anchors that read **11/19 gold edges (58%, Wilson 95% interval about 36–77%)** are therefore below the facet benchmark but not far below it. On 19 items, differences of one or two edges between methods are noise. Second, the facet result holds on unseen domains, which suggests that matching against label text transfers better than models tuned per label. Third, Expressed/Contradicted/Unaddressed is a ready label scheme for the 19 gold answers, with a published human-agreement figure to compare against.

Graph-alignment ASAG supplies the training template. **Mohler, Bunescu & Mihalcea (2011)** score every reference-graph node against every student-graph node and train that node scorer with an averaged perceptron on only "a small set of manually aligned graphs". They then use Hungarian matching with question demoting, which discounts words copied from the question. Finally, SVMRank or SVR combines the alignment features into a grade trained on overall scores. On the Data Structures (CS) dataset, the alignment score alone gave r = 0.41–0.43, and the hybrid gave **r = 0.518**, against inter-annotator agreement of r = 0.586 ([Mohler et al. 2011](https://aclanthology.org/P11-1076.pdf)). Sultan, Salazar & Sumner (2016) raised this to **r = 0.592–0.63**. Their features came from an unsupervised monolingual word aligner, and removing the alignment features dropped r to 0.519 ([Sultan et al. 2016](https://aclanthology.org/N16-1123.pdf)). The design pattern of a few hand-aligned examples for the element scorer plus overall grades for the aggregator maps exactly onto 19 edge-gold answers plus 365 overall-level answers. No source found applies facet entailment to programming explanations.

## AutoTutor already matches pairs of expectations and misconceptions deterministically

AutoTutor's expectation–misconception-tailored design is the closest analogue to edges with a wrong-belief text and a correct-rule twin. Each question has a hand-authored curriculum script of expectations and "bad answers". Each student turn is matched to them by **thresholding LSA cosine**, and the next dialogue move is chosen from those cosines ([Penumatsa et al. 2003](https://cdn.aaai.org/FLAIRS/2003/Flairs03-082.pdf)). Three findings carry over directly. Expectations that experts rated *explicitly present* had a mean cosine of only **0.42**, far below the 0.65 production threshold. Both expectation length and answer length shifted cosines significantly. The score was the **maximum over all combinations of student sentences** ([Penumatsa et al. 2003](https://cdn.aaai.org/FLAIRS/2003/Flairs03-082.pdf)). A follow-up found the best agreement with experts when the threshold depended on both lengths ([Penumatsa et al. 2006](https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/facpubs/7460)). The later engine combined LSA with regular expressions on 5,202 response–expectation pairs. The two were only moderately correlated (ρ = .471), and agreement with humans was "fair, but slightly lower" than human–human agreement ([Carmon et al. 2019](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F3330430.3333649); [Carmon 2021](https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/etd/2148)).

Banjade & Rus give the most directly useful later result. Adding learner knowledge level and question difficulty through **Probabilistic Soft Logic** improved correctness inference on DT-Grade by **more than 7 percentage points** over a model that used similarity alone ([Banjade & Rus 2019](https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/facpubs/2545)). This is published evidence that joint inference over context beats deciding each sentence in isolation, which is the bet ES-Viterbi makes. The caution from SemEval-2013 is that only about half of the submitted systems beat the lexical-overlap baseline. On unseen questions and domains, the best systems scored about 0.49 and 0.47 weighted F1 against the lexical baseline's 0.402 and 0.396 ([Dzikovska et al. 2013](https://aclanthology.org/S13-2045)). Strong lexical retrieval, which TF-IDF anchors already provide, should be kept as the backbone, not replaced.

## Wrong-side versus right-side needs NLI margins, not cosine or pair fine-tuning

Three label-description methods are encoder-only and deterministic. **Entailment-as-classification** treats the text as premise and the label as hypothesis ([Yin, Hay & Roth 2019](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1404/)). Its fully-unseen-label results are modest: a topic accuracy of 37.9–45.7. Long definitions alone performed poorly as hypotheses, so short, canonical edge sentences are better than long "way" descriptions ([Yin et al., ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.00161)). **Label-description training** fine-tunes on a few descriptions and paraphrases of each label rather than on labelled texts. It was **17–19 points absolute more accurate than zero-shot** on topic and sentiment tasks ([Gao, Ghosh & Gimpel 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.02239)). **SetFit** matched 3B-parameter T-Few on average (62.3 vs 63.4) with 8 examples per class using a 110M-parameter encoder ([Tunstall et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.11055v1)). For general NLI backbones, Laurer et al. released DeBERTa-v3 models trained on 764k–885k NLI pairs ([model card](https://huggingface.co/MoritzLaurer/DeBERTa-v3-large-mnli-fever-anli-ling-wanli/blob/b3546ea6b0346eb6f8d5d68b13c7dc6d0376b3d7/README.md)). In ASAG, transfer from an MNLI-tuned RoBERTa improved SciEntsBank results and generalisation to unseen domains ([Camus & Filighera 2020](https://www.kom.tu-darmstadt.de/papers/CF20-1.pdf?download)).

The twin-text problem is where most methods fail. Bi-encoder cosine gives high similarity to minimal negation pairs. Negation-aware training (NegMPNet, on 68,780 rule-generated negation and paraphrase pairs) raised negation sensitivity from **0.14 to 0.65** but antonym sensitivity only from **0.07 to 0.24** ([Anschütz et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.13989)). Java misconceptions often differ by a key word ("compares references" vs "compares contents") rather than by "not", so the weak antonym figure is the one that matters. NLI benchmarks also contain little negation, and state-of-the-art transformers struggle when negation is critical ([Hossain et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.732)). Natural logic is the deterministic answer to exclusion. MacCartney & Manning model antonymy and negation explicitly ([C08-1066](https://aclanthology.org/C08-1066/)), but NaturalLI runs at **91% precision and 49% recall** ([Angeli & Manning 2014](https://aclanthology.org/D14-1059/)). That profile fits an override that fires rarely and is usually right. It does not fit a primary side-decider, which matches what the project saw with its WordNet decider.

The graph-pair-fine-tuned DeBERTa probably failed for a reason the MAP Kaggle competition makes concrete. A DeBERTa-v3-large classifier scored 0.91–0.92 MAP@3 on test questions it had seen. Its cross-validation grouped by question was **0.29**, because supervised classifiers on a few items learn item-specific surface cues ([model card](https://huggingface.co/lyixuan0718/map-deberta-v3-large-5fold)). Fine-tuning on pairs generated from the same graph used for scoring risks the same leak. Hard negatives do help when they are mined properly: on Banking77, self-mined hard negatives took MAP@25 from 0.788 to 0.838, against 0.069 for zero-shot ([DaoyuanLi2816 repo](https://github.com/DaoyuanLi2816/Kaggle-Eedi-Mining-Misconceptions-in-Mathematics-Silver-Medal)). The practical side rule is **entail(s, W) − entail(s, C)** from a frozen off-the-shelf NLI model, with an abstain band. Natural logic flips a decision only when it detects exclusion. **Restrict candidates to the 2–5 edges attached to the item's concepts**, which was a common winning trick in MAP ([Zenn writeup](https://zenn.dev/mkj/articles/1f374627377474)). MathBERT retrieval alone reached MAP@3 0.83 with small candidate sets ([MiRAGE](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01182v1)). One more Eedi lesson transfers with no run-time LLM: enriching catalogue entries offline with longer explanations "improved retrieval performance significantly" for an untuned MiniLM ([Eedi](https://www.eedi.com/news/from-wrong-answers-to-real-insights-how-we-used-a-kaggle-challenge-to-map-student-misconceptions)). Authored paraphrases of each W and C text are therefore a cheap gain. Still, no published evaluation of NLI-based discrimination between correct rule and misconception on programming explanations exists.

## Level decoding collapsed because its evidence vanished, so recalibrate the emissions

The drop in levels from **16/19 (Wilson about 62–94%) to 5/19 (about 12–49%)** is far larger than any change in edge reading, and the two intervals do not overlap. The likeliest reading is that the L0–L4 emissions were built on execution-derived evidence and no longer receive comparable signal. A better side-decider will not fix that. The literature offers text-only level evidence and a way to calibrate it. For code explanations, the SOLO distinction between multistructural and relational responses (line-by-line description versus overall purpose) is the standard level axis ([Whalley et al. 2006, as cited in Smith et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.12216)). Smith, Fowler, Denny & Zilles segment each explanation, map segments to code lines and threshold the segment count. They reach **81% agreement, κ = 0.61** with humans, who agreed with each other at κ = 0.80–0.83 ([Smith et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.12216)). Their segmenter is an LLM. A deterministic proxy could count distinct code lines or identifiers the answer references against purpose verbs such as "returns the largest" or "counts", but that proxy is untested. Non-LLM EiPE grading reached **87–89% accuracy**, comparable to TAs ([Fowler et al. 2021](https://lagrange.mechse.illinois.edu/pubs/FoChAzWeZi2021/)), though it is reported to need substantial labelled data per question ([Smith et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.12216)).

The data budget rules out per-item supervised level scoring. Learning-progression scoring in science reached 72–98% accuracy, but with **361–848 human-scored responses per item** ([Kaldaras & Haudek 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2022.968289/full)). The project has 365 answers in total. Those 365 overall labels can, however, fit a handful of global emission parameters, such as per-level thresholds on edge-coverage counts, a SOLO-proxy weight and a misconception penalty. This follows Mohler's division of labour between an element scorer and an aggregator trained on overall grades ([Mohler et al. 2011](https://aclanthology.org/P11-1076.pdf)), and Levy's rule from facets to a whole answer ([Levy et al. 2013](https://aclanthology.org/P13-2080.pdf)). For the graph layer, Andes builds its Bayesian network from the solution graph with rule-mastery nodes ([Conati, Gertner & VanLehn 2002](https://www.cse.unr.edu/~sjose/Papers%20Referenced/Using%20Bayesian%20Networks%20to%20Manage%20Uncertainty%20in%20Student%20Modeling.pdf)). BKT is itself a two-state HMM per skill with guess and slip parameters ([Corbett & Anderson 1995](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01099821)). Both support parameters set by hand when data are scarce. Unsupervised HMM states are identifiable only up to permutation ([arXiv 2604.16900](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.16900)), so emissions must stay anchored to named edge matchers rather than being learned freely. Where a soft-logic layer is preferred to Viterbi, PSL's convex MAP inference is exact and deterministic ([Bach et al. 2017](https://jmlr.org/papers/v18/15-631.html)). Its one education result in this area is Banjade & Rus's gain of more than 7 points ([Banjade & Rus 2019](https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/facpubs/2545)).

## Conclusion

The ranked shortlist for this exact problem has seven entries. First is **Nielsen/SemEval-2013 facet entailment with UKP-BIU voting**, which is the published form of "judge each graph edge against student text". Second is **AutoTutor EMT matching**, which supplies length-dependent thresholds, a max over sentence combinations and regex plus similarity for twin texts. Third is a **frozen zero-shot NLI cross-encoder used as a W−C margin** (Yin et al.; Laurer DeBERTa). Fourth is **Mohler-style element scoring from a few gold alignments plus an aggregator trained on overall grades**. Fifth is **Banjade & Rus PSL context inference**. Sixth is **Andes/BKT hand-parameterised graph inference** as the Viterbi layer. Seventh is **label-description training or SetFit with forced W/C hard negatives**, held in reserve for when more edge gold exists. The concrete recommendation follows from these. Keep TF-IDF anchors and add the UKP-style Exact and Java-lexicon votes, restricting candidates to the item's own edges. Make every misconception edge a three-way Expressed-correct / Expressed-wrong / Unaddressed decision. Base that decision on a frozen MNLI DeBERTa margin between the W and C texts, with an abstain band, and let the WordNet natural-logic decider flip a decision only on detected exclusion. Drop the graph-pair fine-tune unless it is evaluated with folds grouped by question.

The highest-leverage work is not in edge reading at all. It is rebuilding level emissions from text-only features: edge-coverage counts, a deterministic SOLO proxy and misconception flags. Fit their few parameters on the 365 overall-level answers, and keep the 19 edge-gold answers strictly for evaluation. Two caveats follow. Nineteen answers cannot separate methods that differ by one or two edges, so report Wilson intervals. And the absence of any prior system that decodes named misconception and level paths from text means ES-Viterbi is genuinely novel there, so its results cannot be benchmarked against a published number. The nearest yardstick is the roughly 0.8 per-facet F1 that non-LLM entailment reached on science answers.
