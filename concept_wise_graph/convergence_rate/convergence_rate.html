Rule typeRule
E folk-meaning ruleEveryday "converge" means arrive at the answer; numerically the vector only gets closer with each iteration and stops changing in the printed digits, never reaching the limit exactly.
M wrong rule / right ruleOneIterationSuffices: wrong: One vector-matrix multiplication gives the exact page ranks. | right: Accuracy grows with iterations: about 20 multiplications give two decimal places for tiny.txt and 20 more give three.
T uneven-application ruleApplies LT (run with T, then with more T, compare digits) unevenly: e.g. comparing a run with T = 20 to one with T = 21 and concluding that unchanged digits are accurate.
N identity ruleA performance measure: the number of iterations a method needs to reach a given number of accurate decimal places.
S declarative ruleThe power method needs about 20 multiplications for two decimal places and another 20 for three on tiny.txt, against tens of thousands and millions of moves for RandomSurfer; Brin and Page report 50 to 100 iterations for the web.
LT operational sequencing ruleRun Markov with increasing T (20, 40, ...) and compare the printed vectors; the digits that stop changing are the converged ones.
OP evaluation / result ruleMarkov 20 and 40 agree to two decimals (0.27245 vs 0.27303) and the 40-iteration vector is accurate to three.
OS state-transition ruleEach additional iteration shrinks the error by roughly a constant factor determined by the second largest eigenvalue of P, so a fixed number of iterations adds about one digit.
R composition rule (with a neighbour)with power_method: T in Markov is the iteration count whose size the convergence rate fixes: about 20 per decimal digit for tiny.txt.
P plan / role ruleChoose the stopping point (iteration count) for an iterative method so that results are accurate to the needed digits without wasted work.
C conservation ruleDoubling T from 20 to 40 keeps the first two digits and adds one; changing the leap probability or the graph changes the rate (it depends on lambda2).
D error rulerule broken: Enough iterations must be run for the required accuracy. (silent logic error)
X applicability ruleUse convergence measurements whenever an iterative or sampling method must be stopped; it explains why the power method (about 20 iterations per digit) is preferred to simulation (10x moves per digit).
